I like networking - it's probably my favorite part of being a sysadmin.  While it often doesn't get the attention that the operating system and software stacks do, it is the underpinning of almost everything we do these days, yet it remains virtually invisible to everyday Internet travelers.  However, nothing on the Internet would be possible if it weren't for the ability to pass packets around the globe at the speed that we do.  But networking at an enterprise scale has traditionally had a high entry point.  The gear often cost a fortune (yeah Cisco, I'm looking at you) and the knowledge required to know and understand acronyms such as TCP, ICMP, PSPF, BGP, VRRP, yada, yada, yada, and more importantly, how to implement them properly also had a high barrier to entry.<!--more-->But a lot of that is changing these days.  HP's ProCurve line is making enterprise networking more affordable, especially in the long run due to their lifetime "free" warranty on many of their switching products.  When you add the Cisco SmartExtortion, er, SmartNet onto their already overpriced gear you can quickly spend as much on your network as you would for a well outfitted new car.  Sure, there has always been inexpensive gear from manufacturers such as Netgear, SMC, and D-Link to name a few.  But try to run even a mid-sized distributed L3 network with those brands and you'll quickly find yourself hitting the wall.  I've been bit too many times by the "advanced features" not working as they should with gear from those brands.

Let's also not forget Mikrotik.  They have a really compelling product range nowadays and you can't beat their prices for the feature set that comes out of the box.  Mikrotik devices are the Swiss Army knives of networking, handling routing, switching, firewalling, and even wireless, often in the same device.  And you can set almost every setting using the CLI or the web-based GUI.  So the learning curve is not as steep as it might be for the more traditional enterprise products from HP or Cisco.  The caveat with Mikrotik is the annoying little bugs and the proprietary tweaks that get in the way of the devices being the perfect little engines that could. Most of the bugs and tweaks are not deal breakers, but they are enough to sometimes make you throw up your arms in frustration.
