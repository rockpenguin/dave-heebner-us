So what is the future for [Zimbra](http://www.zimbra.com/)?  Since nobody reads my blog, I'm not sure if I'll ever get an answer :-)

<!--more-->

_Disclaimer: I am a big fan of Zimbra and have used it for my own purposes since about 2005 and even used it commercially for one of the companies I worked for.  If I could, I'd replace every install of Exchange out there with Zimbra._

Zimbra is a "collaboration" mail server, a la Exchange or Notes with the exception that it is easier to setup and maintain.  It has a great web interface - so much so that I don't even use a mail client anymore.  I couldn't even tell you what Thunderbird looks like these days or which version it is.  That's how good the web UI is on Zimbra.  And did I mention that the web UI is the same in pretty much every browser?  Yep, none of that annoying "looks better in IE than Firefox".  Granted MS has made some good progress with Exchange/OWA 2010, but as they say, too little, too late.  The other thing that's great about Zimbra is that they have a "community" open source version with binary installs for CentOS/RH/Ubuntu.  It lacks some of the features of the [paid-for version](http://www.zimbra.com/products/zimbra-collaboration/compare-products.html), but since I only have a few people on my mail server and they are all happy with the web UI then there isn't much impetus for me to upgrade.  That being said, if the price was right I would happily shell out some dough to buy Zimbra as some of the features would be cool to have (real-time backup, clustering/HA).

But I could drone on and on.  My reason for writing this post is that I am worried about the future of Zimbra.  It's had a bit of a [roller coaster ride](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbra) in terms of history, having been acquired by Yahoo, then VMware, then Telligent most recently.  While each acquisition brought some trepidation, development seemed to stay at a steady pace and the open-source version remained an option and each parent company seemed to keep everything intact.  While it's good to see that the software is in the hands of one company whose only products are the ones centered around Zimbra, I have noticed some subtle differences that worry me such as the removal of the ability to purchase the software directly through the website.  While I know that many companies play that game so that you have to call and talk to an annoying sales person, it bothers me because that also usually means that the product has gone up in price (or will).  I guess I don't have much right to complain since I am not currently a paying customer.

The point I am trying to make here is that I would like to see Zimbra gain more ground in the enterprise collaboration market as I think it is hands down the best product out there (don't remind me of Betamax vs. VHS).  One of the reasons I've always had a tough time selling the idea to business folks to throw Exchange out the door is that the price between the two is really not that different, especially for smaller companies. Granted there is the "hidden" costs for Exchange that eventually make it more expensive, but that stuff usually doesn't matter to management.  The IT Dept's pain is not much of a concern to the business folks.  So Exchange stays because it's the easy choice.

Another reason I worry about Zimbra is that email and collaboration platforms have become a commodity with the likes of Gmail and hosted Exchange.  As we move more to the cloud and the IT depts and their respective budgets shrink, having someone else host your mail makes a lot of sense given the hassle of managing a server, worrying about getting hacked, and dealing with the deluge of spam.
